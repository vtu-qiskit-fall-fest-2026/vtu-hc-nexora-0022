Where should limited cooling go first?

Extreme heat reaches every part of a campus, but fans, misting sets and shaded rest areas are limited. HEATSHIELD-Q picks which zones get cooling by weighing population, heat risk and what each zone needs, using a QAOA circuit, and checks the answer against classical methods. Change the scenario and the circuit re-runs.

How the problem becomes a circuit

The cooling choice is written as an energy function. QAOA looks for the bit pattern with the lowest energy.

  1. Pick zones with bits. Qubit i is 1 when zone i gets cooling. Six zones need six qubits.
  2. Reward exposure covered, punish overspending. With spare budget h = B − Σcᵢxᵢ: f(x) = −Σ vᵢ xᵢ − L1·h + L2·h² Going over budget (h < 0) costs quadratically. No extra qubits are needed for the budget.
  3. Convert to an Ising Hamiltonian with x = (1 − Z)/2. Single-qubit fields and two-qubit couplings are shown on the right.
  4. Build the circuit. Start in an equal superposition, then repeat a cost layer and a mixer layer.
  5. Optimise the angles on a classical computer, measure the circuit many times and keep the best allocation within budget.

Penalty weights in this run: . They are chosen so that the lowest-energy state is the true optimum.

What this shows
A complete QAOA workflow for a resource allocation problem: formulation, Hamiltonian, circuit, optimisation, readout and a classical comparison.

      
What it does not show
At 6 zones, brute force takes microseconds, so there is no quantum speed-up here. The value is in the method, which carries over to larger campuses and real devices.

      
Reading the numbers
Exposure values come from the sample inputs on this page. QAOA picks up the optimum several times more often than random guessing, and 1,024 shots are enough to find it.
